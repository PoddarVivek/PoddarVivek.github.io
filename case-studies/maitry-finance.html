Making a lender easy to understand

Maitry Finance, an RBI-registered NBFC in Kolkata, had a dead website. Borrowers couldn't tell what it offered, and there was no easy way to get in touch.

Role
Solo designer and developer
Client
Maitry Finance Limited
Built with
Next.js, Supabase
Scope
Website, product pages, enquiry capture

The problem

The company's old site was effectively dead. It generated no leads, and a visitor couldn't work out which kinds of loans were on offer or who to talk to.

For a lender, that silence costs more than traffic. People looking for a loan are often stressed and wary of hidden charges. If a site doesn't explain the product clearly, they go to a lender whose site does.

My goals were to explain every product — what it is, why you'd pick it, when it fits and how to apply — and to make contacting the team take one tap from anywhere on the site.

Before and after

Before

  • No working website
  • No leads coming in online
  • Loan products not explained anywhere
  • No clear way to reach the team

After

  • Live site with a page for each loan
  • Every product explained: what, why, when, how
  • Contact options in the nav, ticker and every section
  • Enquiries stored in Supabase for client tracking

Design decisions

Name loans by the moment, not the jargon

The three products are framed by the borrower's situation: “for the big plans”, “for right-now moments” and “for the small stuff”. Each card lists real use cases. A fourth card, “Not sure which fits?”, sends undecided visitors straight to the team.

People arrive with a need, not a product name.

Put trust signals before the pitch

The RBI registration number, “no penal interest”, the Key Facts Statement promise and the cooling-off window sit in a scrolling strip at the very top and again in a “lending playbook” section.

Borrowers' biggest fear is hidden costs, so answer it first.

Let people do the maths themselves

An EMI calculator on the homepage lets visitors move amount, tenure and rate sliders and see the monthly EMI and total interest, clearly marked as illustrative.

A visitor who has run the numbers sends a better enquiry.

Explain the process in four steps

“Tell us what you need → verify digitally → get a decision → money in your account” shows the whole journey, including that no branch visit is needed.

Unknown process = drop-off.

Make contact impossible to miss

The phone number is in the top strip, “Get in touch” is in the navigation, and every major section ends with a way to reach the team.

The whole brief was: easy and quick to contact.

Data and what's next

Enquiries are stored in Supabase so the client can track incoming leads. The data is confidential and used only for that purpose. The site replaced a dead page, and enquiries that previously had nowhere to go are now captured.

Next, I'd like to measure which product pages lead to the most enquiries and whether calculator users convert better, then use that to reorder the homepage.
