Answering every call a clinic misses

Independent clinics in India lose patients when the phone rings during a consultation. ClinicAI is an AI receptionist that answers in English, Hindi and Bengali, books into the clinic's real schedule and passes the doctor only the calls that need a decision.

Role
Solo: product, design and build
Type
Personal project, in early access
Built with
Next.js, Supabase
Scope
Landing page, onboarding, clinic workspace, patient portal

The problem

A doctor running a small clinic is usually the busiest person in it. When they're with a patient, the phone rings out, and the patient on the other end simply calls the next clinic. Front-desk staff, where there are any, spend the day firefighting bookings instead of looking after the people in the waiting room.

Patients want something simple: a time confirmed in one call, in their own language, and a way to change it later without calling again.

The hard part isn't answering the phone. It's letting software take bookings without the doctor feeling they've lost control of their own schedule.

Who it's for

The doctor

Owns the clinic and buys the product

Wants the phone answered and bookings made while they're with a patient.

Worries about: losing control of the schedule, or the AI saying something wrong.

Front-desk staff

Use it every day

Want to see who's coming and what needs a decision when the day starts.

Worries about: another piece of software to learn.

The patient

Calls the clinic

Wants a confirmed time in one call, in their language, and to change it later without calling.

Worries about: being put on hold, or forgetting the time.

Design decisions

The doctor stays in control

The AI only books inside the clinic's own rules: its sessions, slot lengths and days off. Anything outside them becomes a request the doctor approves or declines with one tap.

The biggest fear was losing the schedule, so the AI never gets the final say on exceptions.

Show what was done before showing settings

The Today view opens with outcomes, like “the AI answered 9 calls and booked 6”, and only then offers controls. Deciding the day's requests takes about two minutes.

Value first, controls second.

Activation is a real booking

Onboarding has five steps: clinic, hours, how the AI talks, a test call, then the phone line. The test call books a slot into the clinic's own schedule, with a target of under 10 minutes from sign-up.

Trust comes from seeing it work on your own calendar.

One next action per screen

Every page has a single primary button, so staff always know what to do next without training.

Staff didn't want another tool to learn.

Use the clinic's words

The product says slot, day off and front desk, never configuration or entities. Sample data and estimates are labelled as such.

If it sounds like their clinic, it feels like theirs.

Patients change bookings without calling

After the AI books, a patient can sign in at a patient portal with a phone code to see, move or cancel the appointment.

Every change made online is one less call for the clinic.

How I'd measure it

North Star: appointments booked by the AI per clinic per week. It counts both time given back to the clinic and patients kept.

Activation: a new clinic sees a test call book a slot into its own schedule.

Supporting metrics, each tracked as a product event:

  • Sign-up → setup finished → phone line connected
  • Days per week the workspace is opened
  • Time taken to decide a request
  • AI call completion rate: calls that end booked or with an alternative offered
  • Changes patients make in the portal instead of calling

ClinicAI is in early access, so there are no customer numbers yet. These are the numbers I'd watch first.

What's next

SMS or WhatsApp confirmations with a link to the patient portal, one-tap approve notifications for the doctor, staff invites with owner and staff roles, and a weekly summary email built from the same data as the Today view. Pricing still needs to be set before early access ends.
