Why this fixes reviews

Happy eaters stay quiet. Instants give them a reason to talk.

A star rating is homework after a meal. Most people who loved their biryani just eat it and move on, so ratings get shaped by whoever felt strongly enough to type. Instants change the motive: showing good food to your friends is fun, so the good meals finally get recorded, and they send friends straight to the same order.

Parts of this have been tried. Zomato added a friends' food timeline back in 2014, and Venmo grew on a feed of friends' payments that researchers called free advertising. Spotted is different in three ways: it's tied to a live order, it disappears when the food arrives, and it's private until you turn it on.

How Swiggy could prove it in 4 weeks

Who gets it

Whole friend groups, picked at random, rather than individual users. Otherwise people in the test would roast friends who aren't in it, which muddies the result. This is how Aral and Walker ran their trial.

The number that decides it

Orders per user per week, test groups against the rest.

Also watch

Sticker open rate, the share of live orders that get an "Order the same", and Instants per delivered order.

Stop if

Notification opt-outs, uninstalls or switches to Ghost mode rise in the test groups.

Design notes

Why this page works the way it does.

You try it, then you read

Visitors decide whether to stay within about 10 seconds, so the first thing on the page is something to tap rather than a paragraph. NN/g

4 steps, no more

Chameleon found 3-step tours are finished 66% of the time, and completion drops below half after 5 steps. People are also far more likely to finish a tour they started themselves. Chameleon

No dark tutorial overlay

In NN/g's testing, people who went through tutorials rated apps as harder to use, and did no better at the tasks. So there's one green hint on the next thing to tap, and that's it. NN/g

Built for a phone

Most LinkedIn visits come from phones, so on a small screen it's one full-size phone that hands over to your friend's phone when something lands there.
